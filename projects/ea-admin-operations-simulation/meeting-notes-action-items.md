# Meeting Notes → Action Items Exercise

## Scenario

The executive has completed a weekly operations meeting. The notes below contain decisions, requests and follow-ups mixed together. The administrative task is to convert them into a clear action tracker.

## Raw Meeting Notes

**Meeting:** Weekly Operations Review  
**Participants:** Executive, Operations Lead, Sales Lead, Finance Manager, EA  
**Purpose:** Review operational priorities, outstanding client matters and upcoming deadlines.

### Notes

- Finance needs the invoice approval completed before today's payment run.
- The unresolved client escalation must be discussed with the executive before the client call.
- Operations will review the supplier contract renewal and return a recommendation by Monday.
- The final event guest count must be confirmed before the venue deadline tomorrow.
- The conference travel option should be held while the executive confirms the preferred flight.
- Sales Pipeline Review has moved to Thursday at 11:00.
- The EA should circulate the updated calendar invitation and ensure the latest pipeline report is available.
- The executive wants the coaching-call agenda prepared before tomorrow's session.
- A partner introductory meeting should be scheduled for Wednesday at 15:00 if the external party confirms.
- The team 1:1 should be moved to a time that does not conflict with travel planning.

| A003 | Review contract renewal and return recommendation | Operations | Important | Monday | Friday |

| A008 | Prepare coaching-call agenda and notes template | EA | Important | Today | Today |

| A010 | Reschedule team 1:1 without creating a conflict | EA | Important | Tomorrow | Today |

## Administrative reasoning demonstrated

The exercise shows the difference between **meeting notes** and an **action tracker**. A useful administrative record identifies:

1. What must be done
2. Who owns it
3. How important or urgent it is
4. When it is due
5. What it depends on
6. When it should be followed up

This makes the meeting output operational rather than simply descriptive.
